semiotics/absences/

What is missing from the record, and what kind of absence each gap represents. Three distinct propositions are kept separate: not present in this document / not found in the available record / demonstrably did not occur.

Zero evaluations (DFA, 2009–2024)

OBSERVATION The corpus states DFA produced zero evaluations and zero statutory determinations across its full 18-year CA designation period on the submitted OR corpus.
source: Interregnum-nullificans-v13.md §I
INFERENCE Not found in the available record — distinct from demonstrably did not occur. The corpus supplies no primary DFA file record. What the Ombudsman's s.7 powers are being invoked to obtain is precisely this: the DFA internal routing, classification, and response history that would independently verify or complicate the "zero evaluations" characterisation.
See also: behaviour/non-actions/

Rebuttal of 3 June 2025 — unacknowledged for 8 months

OBSERVATION The corpus states a formal rebuttal of the OEP's June 2025 adequacy conclusion was submitted on 3 June 2025 and went unanswered in the 8 months before the McEntee Foreclosure issued on 4 February 2026. The Foreclosure cited the OEP conclusions without acknowledging the rebuttal's existence.
source: Interregnum-nullificans-v13.md §IV; feasible-region.html §06
INTERPRETATION The absence of the rebuttal from the Foreclosure is what the corpus frames as the key irrationality ground under Meadows — the reasons given were inadequate because they omitted a formally-lodged challenge to their primary source. The semiotic move is to treat an absence (no mention of the rebuttal) as proof of a failure rather than as a possible administrative oversight. Whether that move holds under judicial review is the court's question.
See also: behaviour/closures/

No handover documentation (DFA → DETE, 22 Aug 2024)

OBSERVATION The corpus states: "no public record of formal handover documentation for the 2006–2024 unprocessed submission history at the moment the designation transferred."
source: feasible-region.html §07; DETE-letter-Sep-10_clean.md §I
fsd:AdministrativeAmnesia (FSD-Ontology-2026 §4.4)
Defined as: the structural condition in which successive officers encounter a submission as if for the first time, without awareness of prior engagement or accumulated obligation. Distinct from fsd:DesignedForgetting (which is active deletion). The 2024 transfer is the corpus's primary instance.
source: FSD-Ontology-2026.md §4.4
INTERPRETATION "No public record" is not the same as "the record does not exist." The absence may reflect: (a) the transfer genuinely occurred without an audit; (b) documentation exists but is internal and not publicly available; or (c) a FOI or Ombudsman process has not yet surfaced it. The corpus treats (a) as established; this repository treats the distinction between (a), (b), and (c) as unresolved pending primary-record access.

JCPP — 18 contacts, zero replies

OBSERVATION The corpus records 18 formal communications to the Joint Committee on Public Petitions between June 2025 and February 2026 with zero replies.
source: Interregnum-nullificans-v13.md §III; meinhardt-initiative-compact-sep10.md
HYPOTHESIS The "silence" figure of 18 contacts with zero replies is consistent with several explanations: (a) the corpus's account of systematic non-engagement; (b) routine secretarial triage of a very high-volume, mass-distributed correspondence campaign; (c) delivery or format issues. No delivery receipts or secretarial routing logs are in this repository. All three explanations produce the same observable output — no reply — and the record does not distinguish between them.

No statutory account of inapplicability — across all committee contacts

OBSERVATION The corpus states that none of the seven Oireachtas committee contact points produced a written statutory account of why the submission fell outside the receiving body's remit and where it should instead be directed.
source: Interregnum-nullificans-v13.md §III; gatekeeping-brief-v4-8b-interim.md §IV
INTERPRETATION The absence of a statutory account of inapplicability is the Gatekeeping Brief's core procedural grievance: each declination closes the encounter without generating a transferable obligation. Semiotically, this absence is the proof of the "jurisdictional evaporation" claim — each node can produce a locally coherent exit without anyone being accountable for the aggregate result.
