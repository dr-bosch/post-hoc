semiotics/metaphors/

Extended figures of speech the corpus uses to make structural arguments concrete. Each entry notes what the metaphor illuminates and what it potentially obscures.

Thermometer / thermostat

OBSERVATION "A feedback loop that can measure but not correct is a thermometer, not a thermostat." Applied to every contact point in the record: signal received, processed into acknowledgement, system unchanged.
source: feasible-region.html §06
INTERPRETATION The metaphor illuminates the distinction between detection and correction in control systems — a genuine OR/systems concept. What it potentially obscures: in control theory, a thermostat fails because the actuator is broken; in the corpus's account, there is no actuator (no statutory coordination mechanism). These are different failure modes. The thermostat metaphor points to a broken correction mechanism; the jurisdictional indeterminacy argument points to no correction mechanism ever having existed. The corpus conflates them, which is strategically effective but analytically imprecise.
See also: semiotics/oppositions/ (Temporal / Jurisdictional)

Binding constraint (OR/operations frame)

OBSERVATION Repeated OR notes across feasible-region.html sections: "The binding constraint is never the largest formal pool — it is the variable the model didn't include." Applied variously to the coordination gap, party discipline, and the absence of a residual rights holder.
source: feasible-region.html §02, §03, §08
INTERPRETATION The Theory of Constraints (Goldratt) application is appropriate — it correctly identifies that adding capacity at non-bottleneck nodes does not improve system throughput. What the metaphor adds is the implication that identifying the binding constraint is the first and most important analytical step, and that the State has not done this. Whether "the coordination gap" is the binding constraint or whether the submission's claim to invoke Reg. 2021/821 is itself the primary contested variable is the question the metaphor elides.

Public good / Pigouvian corrective

OBSERVATION feasible-region.html §09: "Sovereign analytical capacity [...] is a public good: non-excludable [...] and non-rival [...]. Its non-production through non-determination is a classic public goods market failure [...]. The appropriate corrective is Pigouvian: a statutory instrument that internalises the externality of non-determination."
source: feasible-region.html §09 (Pigouvian note)
INTERPRETATION The public goods framing is a move from administrative law to welfare economics — it reframes the corpus's failure-of-evaluation claim as a market failure, which opens the door to a regulatory remedy rather than just a judicial one. The Pigouvian reading is internally consistent: if non-determination creates a negative externality (the public loses access to sovereign analytical capacity) then a statutory instrument that makes non-determination costly to the producing institution is the correct Pigouvian tax. The legislative proposal (the Qualifying Submissions Bill) is precisely that instrument.
See also: posiwid/purpose-inferred-from-behaviour/
