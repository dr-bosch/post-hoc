The Feasible Region
of a Constitution

Ireland's institutional architecture read as a constraint-satisfaction problem — and the 19-year record of non-evaluation that tests every claim the text makes about itself.

What this is about — for anyone

The story in plain terms

In 2006, an Irish researcher submitted a body of work to the Irish State. The work dealt with Operations Research — mathematical methods for optimising complex systems like energy grids, emergency planning, and national logistics — and it had recognised dual-use characteristics, meaning it had both civilian and security applications. Under EU law, that triggered a specific legal obligation: the State was required to formally evaluate it.

Nineteen years later, it has not done so. Not a positive evaluation. Not a negative one. Not a formal refusal. Nothing. Five governments. Six Prime Ministers. The submission is still waiting. This page is a constitutional analysis of how that is possible — using the same analytical tools that should have been applied to the submission in the first place.

The consequences are concrete: Storm Éowyn (January 2025), the largest infrastructure failure in Irish history, arrived without the emergency planning framework the corpus contained. Energy bills reflect pricing decisions made without the network optimisation models it provided. And Ireland is about to chair EU meetings on the exact topic at the centre of this case, while an EU-mandated compliance review opens in September 2026. The record — the full 19-year record — is now before courts and institutions that cannot be administratively managed.

Three dimensions of the constraint-satisfaction failure

Capital

Who holds the determination. The competent authority designation under Reg. 2021/821 produced zero evaluations across eighteen years of DFA designation. Transferred to DETE on 22 August 2024 without any public record of the unprocessed submission history being disclosed to the receiving authority.

Cogency

What compels compliance. Judicial review is the constraint the Nullification Loop cannot absorb. Three independent irrationality grounds under O'Keeffe. Reasons failure under Meadows. Mandamus to compel evaluation. Certiorari to quash the McEntee Foreclosure of 4 February 2026.

Capaciousness

What the EU layer adds. Arts. 41 and 47 CFR directly effective. Art. 2 TEU rule-of-law obligation engaged. Reg. 2021/821 Art. 26 mandatory evaluation opens 10 September 2026 — within the Presidency window, insulated from domestic administrative filtering.

01
Structure

The Allocation

The Constitution distributes public authority across three pools and is silent on what happens when a cross-departmental submission falls between them. Against this allocation, the O.R. corpus submitted in May 2006 advanced a specific claim: that the designated competent authority was required to evaluate it under EU Regulation 428/2009, subsequently recast as Regulation 2021/821. The DFA held that function for eighteen years without producing a determination. On 22 August 2024, the Control of Exports Act 2023 transferred the designation to DETE without any public record of the unprocessed submission history being disclosed.

The accumulated obligation was not transferred. It was structurally abandoned.

O.R. note The text distributes the function. It does not specify what happens when the function is received and not performed — when every formal step in the administrative process is executed correctly and the substantive obligation goes permanently unmet. The gap between "competent authority designated" and "evaluation produced" is the space this record inhabits.
In plain terms In 2006, a researcher handed the Irish State a body of work. The State said thank you, created a reference number, and filed it. That happened again in 2009, and 2020, and 2024. Each time: receipt confirmed, evaluation not produced. Nineteen years of filing is not nineteen years of consideration. A reference number is not a verdict. The submission is still waiting for one.
Deontological register · five substitutions

The Loop is sustained by five substitutions. Each is individually defensible. The sequence is not. The failure is not one of degree — it is one of kind: each substitution enacts a categorically different act from the one the statutory relationship required.

02
Constraint

The Bottleneck

Executive predominance is the equilibrium state of the Irish constitutional system once party discipline is treated as a variable the text never priced in. The analysis of the Nullification Loop adds a structural variant: not party discipline but the absence of a coordination mechanism between DFA, DETE, and DOD — three institutions that collectively hold the competent authority function under Regulation 2021/821 with no statutory mechanism requiring integrated assessment between them.

Dual-use evaluation requires integration by definition. The post-transfer architecture makes it architecturally impossible — not temporarily deferred, not dependent on political will. The architecture provides no path from "submission received" to "determination issued."

O.R. note The binding constraint is never the largest formal pool — it is the variable the model didn't include. Here it is not party discipline but the coordination gap the 2024 transfer created by distributing the evaluation function across three institutions with no mechanism to assemble it. The solution is architecturally unavailable, not temporarily deferred.
In plain terms When a government controls parliament, parliament tends not to push back very hard — that is executive predominance. But the deeper problem here is different: after a bureaucratic reorganisation in August 2024, three separate departments now share the legal responsibility for evaluating the submission, and none of them has a statutory obligation to coordinate with the others. So the evaluation cannot happen even if everyone wanted it to.

03
Legal-Instrumental

The Unconstrained Check

Judicial review is the one instrument the Nullification Loop cannot absorb. It is being engaged. Three independent irrationality grounds arise from the McEntee Foreclosure of 4 February 2026 under O'Keeffe v An Bord Pleanála [1993]: contested, unanswered conclusions cited as settled authority; no reference to Storm Éowyn despite the OEP's finding of adequacy having been written in that emergency's aftermath; no reference to the competent authority obligation or the 226-page formal complaint. Reasons failure under Meadows [2010] 2 IR 701 applies independently.

O.R. note The strongest constraint in a system isn't the largest pool — it is the one no other actor can borrow against. Judicial review derives its binding force not from how often it is used but from the obligation it places on every decision-maker who knows it is forthcoming. It existed throughout the nineteen years. The Loop did not resolve it. It deferred it.
In plain terms Courts are the one part of the system a government doesn't control. A judge can examine a minister's decision and say "that was unreasonable" or "you didn't give adequate reasons" and strike it down. That is what is being prepared here. In February 2026 a minister closed the matter by citing a disputed document as though it were settled — without mentioning that the dispute had been formally raised eight months earlier and never answered.

04
Analytical

Low-Throughput Nodes

The Joint Committee on European Union Affairs holds a mandate covering EU affairs oversight. Between September 2025 and March 2026, seven contact points formally engaged the JCEUA on a submission naming ten EU-law dimensions. JCEUA-I-57 (7 October 2025) declined jurisdiction. The formally received correspondence was subsequently deleted. No statutory account of inapplicability was produced at any of the seven contact points.

Nominal mandate: fully engaged. Exercised capacity on this matter: nil. The deletion is before the European Ombudsman as a direct violation of Article 41 CFR — the right of access to one's file.

O.R. note A node with full formal capacity and near-zero utilisation isn't idle — it is either a reserve for edge cases the system was designed around, or it has substituted the appearance of oversight for its function. The deletion of the record makes the distinction legally material: not a failure to act, but an act that is itself now reviewable.
In plain terms The parliamentary committee whose job is to scrutinise EU affairs was contacted seven times over seven months. Each time it received a submission engaging EU law. It declined to engage and then deleted the correspondence. Under EU law, you have the right to access documents held about you by public bodies. Deletion removes that right. The European Ombudsman is now investigating.

05
O.R. Domain

Auxiliary Capacity

The Irish Ombudsman (OMB-260331-CSD014), the Public Accounts Committee (S-2026-0307), the European Parliament's PETI committee (Article 227 TFEU petition pending), and the CJEU parallel filing are four auxiliary tracks operating simultaneously alongside the High Court preparation. None can direct Government policy unilaterally. Three can trigger mandatory process: the Ombudsman holds Section 7 powers to extract the DFA internal file; PETI admission triggers mandatory Commission response; Article 26 of Regulation 2021/821 opens a mandatory Commission evaluation on 10 September 2026 regardless of domestic proceedings.

O.R. note Capacity added outside the core allocation increases what the system can monitor and — through the EU tracks — what it can subject to mandatory process. The Art. 26 evaluation is not oversight. It will process Ireland's compliance record at technical desk level during the Presidency window, insulated from domestic administrative filtering.
In plain terms Five separate processes are running at the same time — not as backup plans, but because each one can reach something the others cannot. The Irish Ombudsman can compel the release of documents. The European Parliament committee can trigger a mandatory EU response. The EU compliance review in September 2026 will examine Ireland's record whether the Irish Government engages with it or not.

06
Narrative

Weak Feedback

The OEP's June 2025 finding of adequacy was issued five months after Storm Éowyn, from inside the emergency's operational aftermath. It concluded that O.R. methodologies were already in use and the OEP possessed a good understanding of Stochastic Programming — without any qualitative evaluation of the submitted corpus. The petitioner's formal rebuttal of 3 June 2025 went unanswered for eight months. The McEntee Foreclosure cited the OEP's letter as dispositive without acknowledging the rebuttal existed.

The feedback that should have triggered correction arrived at every stage and was processed into acknowledgement. Every contact point. Across nineteen years. At none of them did the system adjust.

O.R. note A feedback loop that can measure but not correct is a thermometer, not a thermostat. Every signal the record generated — four formal contact points spanning 2006–2025, a ministerial commitment in 2009, Storm Éowyn itself — was received and processed into acknowledgement. The loop measured at every stage. It corrected at none.
In plain terms Storm Éowyn knocked out power to hundreds of thousands of Irish homes in January 2025. Five months later, the emergency planning office said it already understood the relevant methods and had no need to evaluate the submission. It said this while still managing the aftermath of the emergency those methods were designed to help prevent. A court will be asked whether that finding was reasonable.

07
Neutral

The Operational Layer

The post-transfer architecture distributes the evaluation function across DFA (international regime memberships), DETE (licensing), and DOD (security assessment architecture), with no statutory coordination mechanism between them. An integrated assessment — which dual-use evaluation requires by definition — is architecturally impossible within this structure. There is no public record of formal handover documentation for the 2006–2024 unprocessed submission history at the moment the designation transferred.

The operational layer received a designation without the history that should have accompanied it. A handover that copies the title without the record does not transfer the obligation. It manufactures a structural amnesia.

O.R. note Every allocation problem needs an execution layer. Ireland's now holds the designation across three institutions without statutory coordination. The submission that requires an integrated assessment from all three simultaneously has no architectural path through which that assessment can be assembled.
In plain terms When the legal responsibility shifted from one department to another in August 2024, there is no public record of the 19-year history — the submissions received, the commitments made, the evaluations not produced — being included in the handover. The new department doesn't formally know what the old department received and failed to act on. This is what structural amnesia means in practice.

08
Process · Chain Flow

The Exogenous Constraint

Ireland assumes the rotating EU Council Presidency on 1 July 2026, chairing the precise Council formations governing dual-use technology controls — the exact domain of the compliance failure this record documents. The Commission's mandatory Article 26 evaluation of Regulation 2021/821 opens 10 September 2026: within the Presidency window. Ireland's compliance record — zero evaluations across eighteen years of DFA designation, transfer-without-discharge on 22 August 2024, and a three-institution architecture the Commission's January 2024 White Paper identifies as structurally deficient — will enter the evaluation dossier at technical desk level as an unremediated, systemic breach.

O.R. note A constraint imposed from outside the original feasible region doesn't just add a rule — it shrinks the region the domestic branches were solving inside. The Art. 26 evaluation is mandatory. It cannot be filtered domestically. The Presidency does not resolve the paradox. It makes it structurally visible to every counterpart at the Council table.
In plain terms Ireland takes over the chair of EU meetings on 1 July 2026 — including meetings on the exact type of export controls at the centre of this case. At the same time, a mandatory EU review of those rules opens in September 2026 and will examine Ireland's compliance record. That examination cannot be stopped by any Irish government department. It will proceed whether or not the domestic case has been resolved.

For everyone · concrete consequences

What this means

This is not an abstract legal dispute. Four things in ordinary Irish life are directly shaped by the 19-year gap this record describes.

Energy
Your electricity bill
EirGrid decides how power is priced and dispatched across the national grid. The O.R. corpus contained optimisation models for exactly this kind of network. Without those models being evaluated, Irish energy pricing has proceeded for 19 years without the independent benchmark they would have provided. Bills reflect decisions made in the absence of available analytical tools.
Safety
Storm Éowyn
January 2025. Hundreds of thousands of households without power. The largest infrastructure failure in Irish history. The O.R. corpus contained stochastic programming methods for exactly this class of emergency — planning under uncertainty, quantifying risk, optimising responses. Those methods were in State custody for 19 years. The storm arrived without them.
Democracy
Who answers
Nineteen years spans five governments. It is long enough for a commitment made by a minister in 2009 to still be outstanding in 2026 when that minister is Taoiseach. The record documents not a bureaucratic error but a governance system that, in its normal operation, produced no outcome at all. That is what the record exists to make visible.
Public goods
Who benefits
The O.R. methods in the corpus could be available to every emergency coordinator, energy planner, and infrastructure decision-maker in the country. 19 years of non-evaluation has kept them in administrative custody. The question is not whether they are valuable. It is who gets to decide when the public has access to analytical tools submitted to the State on the public's behalf.
09 · Synthesis · The Feasible Region Finding

The Stable Pathology

Strong judicial independence Weak executive/legislative separation CA architecture without coordination Rising EU constraint Presidency Paradox · 1 Jul 2026

Ireland's constitutional architecture produces executive predominance as its stable equilibrium once party discipline is treated as a real-world constraint. The Nullification Loop is the additional stable equilibrium the architecture produces when applied to a cross-departmental submission requiring integrated assessment: no single institution holds both the evaluative obligation and the evaluative capacity simultaneously; no feedback loop holds sufficient correction authority; no coordination mechanism exists between the three institutions that collectively hold the function; and the EU exogenous constraint is entering a mandatory evaluation the domestic architecture cannot filter.

The Loop is not a malfunction. It is the architecture's stable output when applied to exactly the class of problem this submission represents — when no node in the system holds the obligation to recognise that the collective function has not been performed. The feasible region never contained a domestic solution. The record is now before courts and institutions that do not require one.

Public good economics · the Pigouvian reading

Sovereign analytical capacity — the State's independent ability to evaluate and deploy methodology — is a public good: non-excludable (if the State evaluates independently, all citizens benefit from better public decisions) and non-rival (one evaluation does not reduce the capacity for another). Its non-production through non-determination is a classic public goods market failure: the cost of evaluation falls on a single submitter; the benefit is non-excludable across the population; no individual node captures enough of the collective benefit to make evaluation individually rational. The Nullification Loop is the mechanism by which that equilibrium is preserved. The appropriate corrective is Pigouvian: a statutory instrument that internalises the externality of non-determination into the decision-making calculus of the bodies that produce it. That instrument is before the Seanad.

The same problem · different register

When the Feasible Region Has No Time

Swap "competent authority" for "field coordinator" and "evaluation function" for "logistics dispatch" and the architecture described in §09 is the standard operating condition of humanitarian response: mandate without integrated command, coordination bodies with audit authority but no directive power, and an accountability chain legally fixed on one actor while execution disperses across a dozen. The emergency logistics register — sandstone, coral, satin, scarlet — exists because the constraint-satisfaction problem is the same one, under time pressure and with worse data.

Storm Éowyn made this concrete. The analytical framework for that class of event was in State custody. It arrived without it. Nineteen years of non-evaluation privatised the benefit to incumbents who gain from the absence of independent benchmarking, while denying it to the public — the households, the emergency coordinators, the infrastructure planners — who were the constitutional beneficiaries of an evaluation the State undertook to produce.

The corpus was formally submitted in May 2006. The obligation to evaluate it is still outstanding. The record of its non-evaluation is now public, indexed in fourteen EU languages, and before institutions whose processes cannot be closed by administrative filing.
