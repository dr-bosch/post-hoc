Constitutional Foundation · Article 40.3 · from the constitutional map

Article 40.3

Defend and vindicate — positive duty. Three independent grounds. Three different remedies. Not all defeated by the same institutional defence.

The obligation imposed by Article 40.3 of Bunreacht na hÉireann is not passive. The State must "defend and vindicate" the personal rights of the citizen and protect those rights "as best it may." The phrase "as best it may" is a quality standard — not a limiting clause, not an excuse for inaction, not a reference to the limits of the possible. It is a mandate to deploy the best available means toward the vindication of protected rights.

Ground 1 — Property right violation. 26kg of proprietary O.R. materials retained by the State since May 2006 without evaluation, compensation, or lawful basis. The tort of conversion runs continuously from the moment the written commitment to evaluate was suppressed in 2009. Cause of action complete. The failure to return, evaluate, reject, or compensate is not a gap in the administrative record. It is a completed legal injury that compounds annually.

Ground 2 — Structural design failure. The State constructed an architecture — three institutions (DFA, DETE, DOD), no statutory coordination mechanism between them — in which integrated assessment of a dual-use submission is architecturally impossible. The three-institution fragmentation is not merely a regulatory non-compliance. It is a failure to discharge the constitutional obligation "as best it may," because the institutional design makes "best" effort structurally unavailable. This is independently reviewable in the High Court.

Ground 3 — Epistemic substitution. Art. 40.3 encompasses the right of a Principal Investigator to have knowledge submitted to the State processed as knowledge — not as correspondence subject to procedural closure. twenty years of procedural acknowledgement without epistemic engagement is the State's sustained substitution of correspondence processing for knowledge evaluation. Art. 40.3 does not permit that substitution.

Durability under adversarial review
These three grounds are independently sufficient. They address different elements of Art. 40.3, engage different remedies, and are not all defeated by the same institutional defence — which is precisely what makes the threefold structure durable under adversarial review. See Section IV of the Post Hoc Report for the full grounds table.

Constitutional Foundation · Article 45 · from the constitutional map

Article 45

Directive Principles — common good. Non-justiciable in itself. Operative as an interpretive constraint on Art. 40.3. Article 40.3 carries the justiciable load.

Article 45 sets out the Directive Principles of Social Policy. Non-justiciability in itself does not mean irrelevance. Article 45 provides that its principles "shall not be cognisable by any court under any of the provisions of this Constitution." But the principles are operative as interpretive constraints on all justiciable provisions — including Art. 40.3.

The quality standard required by Art. 40.3 — "as best it may" — is measured against what Art. 45 requires: a State that acts for the common good by deploying its best analytical capacity in the service of citizens' protected rights. A State that constructs an administrative architecture structurally incapable of integrated assessment — for the governance of a corpus with demonstrable applications in emergency planning, energy grid optimisation, and national security — is not acting for the common good.

Art. 40.3 carries the justiciable load. Art. 45 shapes the quality standard applied to the State's architectural choices and the measure against which "as best it may" is calibrated.

Article 9.3 + Preamble

Reciprocal civic compact. Citizenship is not a one-way relationship. The Preamble commits to prudent and just social order.

Article 9.3 provides that fidelity to the nation and loyalty to the State are fundamental political duties of all citizens. The Preamble commits the State to seeking to promote the common good, with due observance of prudence, justice and charity, so that the dignity and freedom of the individual may be assured.

The civic compact is reciprocal. Citizens owe fidelity and loyalty; the State owes responsiveness to citizens' formal submissions that engage matters of public importance. The State's failure to assess a submitted corpus formally held in State custody for twenty years — acknowledged verbally at Taoiseach level, committed to at ministerial level, formally engaged by five subsequent governments — is a breach of that compact in the most literal sense.

The Preamble's commitment to "prudent and just social order" supplies a further standard: prudence requires that a State evaluate what it holds before it makes decisions that would be improved by that evaluation. twenty years of non-evaluation across the TMS write-down, Storm Éowyn, and EirGrid pricing is a departure from the prudent governance the Preamble commits to.

Ex Parte Proposition · Institutional Competence · from the constitutional map

Institutional competence

As constitutional imperative. O.R. methodology = deployable means. Failure to deploy is failure of constitutional function, not merely administrative shortcoming.

The ex parte proposition of institutional competence states a constitutional imperative: an institution with the statutory function to evaluate a submission is obliged to deploy its most effective analytical means toward that evaluation. The failure to do so is not merely a procedural shortcoming — it is a failure of institutional competence as a constitutional imperative.

Operations Research methodology constitutes the available analytical means for integrated assessment of complex cross-departmental governance problems. The DFA, as competent authority under EU Regulation 428/2009 from 2009, had both the statutory function and the available means. It deployed neither across eighteen years. DETE, as competent authority from August 2024, has inherited the statutory function without any documented evaluation plan for the unprocessed backlog.

The proposition is not that the State must vindicate the corpus's claims. It is that the State must engage with them. The constitutional imperative of institutional competence requires evaluation, not vindication. An institution that possesses the means to evaluate and declines to use those means has not discharged its constitutional function. It has substituted the appearance of function for function itself. This is the Phainocracy reading applied at the level of the institution rather than the governance system.

Ex Parte Proposition · Fiduciary Steward · from the constitutional map

Fiduciary steward

State holds corpus as trustee. Not gatekeeper — the beneficiary is the public. Non-assessment is breach of fiduciary duty, not neutral administrative outcome.

The fiduciary proposition reconceives the State's relationship to the O.R. corpus. The State is not a gatekeeper — holding the corpus at the threshold of State consideration, empowered to decide whether to engage with it. The State is a fiduciary — holding the corpus as trust property, with active duties of care toward the beneficiary.

The beneficiary is not the petitioner. The beneficiary is the public: the citizens of the jurisdiction who would benefit from the analytical frameworks the corpus contains being evaluated, integrated, and made operationally available. Emergency planning frameworks derived from stochastic programming; energy grid optimisation benchmarks; haulier network resilience models — these are public goods held in State custody and denied to the public through non-assessment for twenty years.

A fiduciary cannot simply acknowledge the receipt of trust property and file it. The fiduciary has an active duty: to evaluate, determine, and either integrate or return — with reasons. twenty years of filing-without-evaluation is not the performance of fiduciary duty. It is its sustained breach.

Evidence-based policy

Non-assessment = non-vindication. Holding available analytical tools in abeyance is a decision not to act as best it may.

Non-assessment = non-vindication: where Art. 40.3 requires the State to act "as best it may" in protecting rights, a decision to hold available analytical tools in abeyance is a decision not to act as best it may.
