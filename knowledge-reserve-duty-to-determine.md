## I. The Proposition in One Sentence

> *A person should be able to submit something of public value to the Irish State and receive an answer — a reasoned, written, reviewable determination. At present, no such guarantee exists in enforceable form. This Bill establishes it as a justiciable obligation.*

---

## II. The Bill — Operative Architecture

### 1. The Core Obligation (§3)
Every public body that receives a **qualifying submission** must issue a **determination**: a written administrative act that either (a) accepts and initiates evaluation, (b) rejects with specific and attributable reasons capable of review, or (c) refers it to a named competent body with a stated legal basis.

### 2. The Definition (§2)
A **qualifying submission** is a written communication that:
- Identifies the submitter;
- Identifies the specific EU instrument or domestic statute to which it is relevant;
- Describes with particularity the technical corpus submitted;
- Is of a kind such that no individual public body has sufficient incentive, in the ordinary course of its own functions, to undertake that evaluation without the duty imposed by this Act.

This definition encodes the **public goods market failure** the Bill addresses: the benefit of evaluation is diffuse and non-excludable; no body captures enough of it to act without a legal duty.

### 3. The Five Prohibited Evasions (§§3–7)
| Substitution | Statutory Form | What It Is |
|---|---|---|
| **Silence** | §3(3)(a), §2A | No response within the statutory period does not constitute a determination. Established by effect, not intent. |
| **Acknowledgement / Remit displacement** | §3(3)(b), §4 | A reference number or auto-reply does not constitute a determination. Claiming the matter "belongs elsewhere" without identifying the competent body and legal basis does not discharge the duty. |
| **Sufficiency assertion** | §3(3)(c), §5 | Claiming general familiarity with the subject domain, or that equivalent methods exist, does not constitute a determination. The body must engage the specific submitted corpus. |
| **Administrative amnesia** | §6 | Treating accumulated prior engagement as a "first encounter" is prohibited. The complete prior record must be consulted and addressed. |
| **Designed forgetting / Record deletion** | §7 | Deleting, destroying, or rendering inaccessible formally lodged correspondence is prohibited. Any such act constitutes reviewable maladministration under the Ombudsman Act 1980. |

### 4. The Two Named Pathologies (§2A)
| Pathology | Definition | Proof Condition |
|---|---|---|
| **Silence veto** | The exercise of a power to determine the effective outcome of a submission through procedural silence, such that no reviewable act issues and no remedy attaches. | Established by **effect**, not intent. Where the practical effect is no reviewable determination, the condition is met regardless of the form of conduct. |
| **Structural displacement** | The distribution of a statutory obligation across bodies in a configuration where no single body holds the full obligation required to discharge it, and no mechanism requires them to act in coordination. | Established by reference to the **statutory architecture**, without requiring proof of coordination between bodies or individual awareness. |

### 5. Testable Submissions & Independent Technical Audit (§§5A–5C)
Where a qualifying submission includes an operational, executable, or empirically testable component, and the submitter has offered the means of testing, the body may not retreat to a sufficiency assertion without first arranging a reasonable opportunity to observe what was submitted.

- An independent technical audit panel is maintained by the Ombudsman.
- The auditor assesses whether the corpus is operational as claimed, whether it produces the outputs claimed, and whether the claimed applications are technically feasible.
- **The auditor does not express a view on whether the body should adopt the submission.** That question remains entirely within the body's discretion.

### 6. Retrospective Application & Chain-of-Duty (§§8–8F)
- **Transitional duty:** Submissions lodged before commencement must be determined within six months of commencement.
- **Chain-of-duty (§8C):** The period for determination runs from the date of **first lodgement** with any body in the chain. It is **not reset** by transfer of designation, ministerial reorganisation, or referral.
- **Written commitments (§8(7)):** Where a written commitment to evaluate, report on, or determine a qualifying submission was made by an officer in their official capacity before commencement, a procedural legitimate expectation arises that the process so committed to will occur.
- **Foreclosure-by-Proxy (§8A):** A determination made by reliance on conclusions that were formally contested and never defended does not constitute a determination under this Act.

### 7. The Loop Closure Mechanism (§§10A–10E)
This is the Bill's most structurally significant provision.

**The loop condition (§10B):** Satisfied where **three or more public bodies** have each declined to examine a qualifying submission on its merits without issuing a determination, and no body has accepted carriage of the determination obligation, over a period of five years.

**The residual obligation (§10C):** Where the loop condition is satisfied, the **Ombudsman** bears a residual determination obligation. The Ombudsman investigates, produces a report, and — where no combination of existing bodies acting within their current statutory powers is capable of producing a determination — identifies the legislative or executive intervention required and transmits that finding to the Minister and to the relevant Oireachtas committee.

**Mandatory High Court application:** The Ombudsman's obligation to apply to the High Court is **not displaced** by the pendency of any other proceeding, any institutional reorganisation, or any subsequent engagement that does not constitute a determination.

**Public register (§10E):** The Ombudsman must maintain a public register of every loop condition satisfied, updated within 30 days of any material change.

### 8. The Savings Clause (§12)
The statutory pathway does not foreclose, and does not require election against, any civil remedy that exists in parallel:
- Conversion cause of action;
- Remedies in tort;
- Remedies for unlawful retention of material submitted in a custodial capacity;
- Fiduciary claims.

A determination issued under this Act is **not** a discharge of the civil wrong. The two tracks run simultaneously.

### 9. Petitions & Proxy Exits (§13)
Transmission to a public petitions committee does **not** satisfy the duty of determination, and does **not** count as a declination for loop-closure purposes unless that committee has declined on the merits in compliance with §10. A petition can no longer function as an exit that quietly leads back into the Loop.

### 10. The AI Act Bridge (§11A)
Creates a reporting obligation connecting the Bill's determination mechanism to high-risk AI conformity assessments under Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. A State that cannot determine whether a submitted technical methodology engages its dual-use obligations cannot evaluate whether it engages its AI conformity obligations.

## III. The Memoranda — Diagnostic Framework

The Memoranda (A through F, and the sequence to the King’s Inns, OPLA, and the Oireachtas) constitute the **diagnostic record** that informed the Bill. They are not grievances appended to the Bill. They are the evidentiary record of the structural condition the Bill addresses.

### Key Concepts from the Memoranda
| Memorandum Concept | Bill Translation | Diagnostic Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Silence Veto** (Memo E) | §2A | The procedural condition where administrative silence operates as an effective veto on civic signal, without requiring proof of bad faith. |
| **Structural Antinomy** (Memo D) | §2A(2) | The condition where two valid statutory duties collide and produce a void; the Bill resolves it by imposing a single, justiciable duty. |
| **Administrative Amnesia / Designed Forgetting** | §6 & §7 | The Loop's production of institutional forgetfulness; each new officer encounters the record as if for the first time. |
| **Sufficiency Assertion** | §3(3) & §5 | The substitution of generic disciplinary familiarity for actual corpus engagement. |
| **Recursive Nullification / The Loop** | §10B–10E | The stable equilibrium of a differentiated system where no single node is required to hold the aggregate obligation. |
| **Chain-of-Duty / Clock Reset** | §8C | The transfer of a competent authority designation without audit of the accumulated obligation does not restart the compliance clock. |
| **Fiduciary Framing** (Memo B–D) | §12 (Savings) | The State as trustee of civic intelligence; the Bill preserves parallel fiduciary remedies. |
| **Cooling the Mark Out** (Memo B) | §3(3) | Goffman's concept applied to institutional neutralisation of disruptive truth through ritualised contradiction. |
| **Séachanachas / Evasocracy** (Memo E) | Entire Bill | The system where governance preserves its power by refusing to fully integrate or cohere; the Bill makes the void illegal. |

### The Meta-Communicative Condition
The memoranda identify a **meta-communicative distortion**: the system imposes a duty to report malfunction but provides no arena in which such reports can compel change. A complaint directed toward inaction is met by the very inaction complained of — under new heading, or by another clerk. Referral replaces judgment. The docket becomes the loop.

This is not merely dysfunction; it is **antinomy**. The system performs legality without recursion; it teaches duty without access to operative levers; it circulates constitutional signals without uptake.

## IV. The Nullification Loop — Structural Analysis

### The Five Substitutions (Invariant Across Personnel, Governments, and Restructurings)
1. **Acknowledgement substitutes for evaluation.**
2. **Deferral without destination substitutes for referral.**
3. **Remit displacement substitutes for jurisdiction.**
4. **Sufficiency assertion substitutes for corpus engagement.**
5. **Designed forgetting substitutes for record.**

Each substitution is individually defensible. The sequence is not. Nineteen years of procedurally valid non-determination produces the same constitutional result as a single unlawful refusal — and no existing instrument reaches the pattern rather than the act.

### The Loop as Public Goods Market Failure
The meta-subject is the economics of public goods:
- **Non-excludability:** Once the State can evaluate independently, everyone benefits from better public decisions.
- **Non-rivalry:** One body's use of the evaluation does not reduce its availability.
- **Market failure:** The benefit is diffuse; the cost falls on a single submitter; no individual node captures enough collective benefit to make evaluation individually rational.

The Bill corrects this by:
1. Imposing a cost on non-determination (Ombudsman process, High Court costs discretion, public register);
2. Creating a residual supply mechanism (Ombudsman's mandatory High Court application) that produces the public good even when no individual body would supply it voluntarily.

This is, in economic terms, a **Pigouvian correction**: a statutory instrument that internalises an externality into the decision-making calculus of the bodies that produce it.

### The Kojèvian Framing
Alexandre Kojève's reading of Hegel identifies recognition (*Anerkennung*) as the structural engine of political obligation. The master seeks recognition from the slave; but the slave's recognition is worthless because the master has already denied the slave the status of a free subject capable of giving meaningful recognition.

The nullification loop is a Kojèvian dyad:
- The State receives the submission but does not engage with it as knowledge — it processes it as correspondence.
- In doing so, it denies the submitter the status of a knowledge-producing subject capable of making a claim that the State is obliged to answer.
- The submitter cannot obtain recognition from an institution that has already, structurally, refused to recognise what is being submitted.

The Bill's loop closure mechanism breaks the dyad by introducing a third party — the Ombudsman, and behind it the High Court — that is not party to the original refusal of recognition.

## V. Comparative Law — French Administrative Law

### What French Law Possesses (Partial Analogies)
| Concept irlandais | Équivalent français | Ce qu'il fait — et ses limites |
|---|---|---|
| **Accusé de réception** | **Art. L. 112-3 CRPA** (loi n° 2000-321 du 12 avril 2000) | L'administration doit accuser réception et indiquer le délai, l'autorité compétente et les voies de recours. **Limite** : pas de mécanisme de boucle. |
| **Transmission à l'autorité compétente** | **Art. 20 loi 2000-321** | Si la demande est adressée à une autorité incompétente, celle-ci *doit* la transmettre à l'autorité compétente et en aviser l'intéressé. Le délai de décision implicite court à compter de la saisine de l'autorité initiale. **Limite** : pas de mécanisme pour le cas où plusieurs autorités se renvoient mutuellement la balle. |
| **Silence de l'administration** | **Art. 21 loi 2000-321, modifié par loi n° 2013-1005** | Le silence vaut désormais *acceptation* au bout de deux mois (avec 1 686 exceptions par décret). Avant 2013, le silence valait rejet. **Limite** : le *ping-pong* administratif n'est pas attrapé — chaque organisme parle, personne ne décide. |
| **Obligation de motivation** | **Loi n° 79-587 du 11 juillet 1979, codifiée dans le CRPA** | Les décisions individuelles défavorables doivent être motivées. Pour les décisions implicites de rejet, l'intéressé peut demander les motifs dans le mois. **Limite** : pas d'interdiction textuelle de l'affirmation de suffisance. |
| **Médiateur / Défenseur des droits** | **Loi organique n° 2011-333 du 29 mars 2011** | Le Défenseur peut émettre des recommandations, des injonctions, et en dernier ressort un *rapport spécial*. Il peut saisir le juge administratif en référé, mais **uniquement** pour obtenir des informations ou l'autorisation de visiter des locaux — **pas pour contraindre l'administration à statuer**. |

### Les quatre ruptures du projet irlandais (où le droit français s'arrête)
1. **La condition de boucle (loop condition)** — Aucun droit français ne nomme le phénomène où trois administrations se renvoient mutuellement la balle sans statuer.
2. **L'Ombudsman *obligé* de saisir la Haute Cour** — Le Défenseur des droits ne peut pas contraindre l'administration par voie juridictionnelle.
3. **L'interdiction des affirmations de suffisance** — Nulle part l'argument « nous connaissons déjà ce domaine » n'est expressément prohibé comme substitut à l'examen au fond.
4. **L'audit technique indépendant** — Aucune disposition législative équivalente n'existe dans le CRPA.

## VI. Comparative Law — EU Member States

### Constitutional Right to Good Administration / Reply
| Pays | Disposition | Ce qu'elle garantit — et ses limites |
|---|---|---|
| **Portugal** | **Art. 268, Constitution** (révisions successives, dernière en 2005) | Droit d'être informé de l'avancement de la procédure ; accès aux dossiers ; décisions motivées ; la loi fixe un délai maximal pour les réponses de l'Administration. **Limite** : pas de mécanisme de boucle, pas de renvoi obligatoire au juge. |
| **Lettonie** | **Art. 104, Constitution** (réinst. 1991, rév. 2014) | Tout le monde a le droit de présenter des soumissions aux institutions de l'État et de recevoir une réponse substantielle (materiāli atbildīgu atbildi). **Limite** : pas de définition juridique de la « boucle », pas de pouvoir coercitif du médiateur devant les tribunaux. |
| **Hongrie** | **Art. XXIV, Loi fondamentale** (2011) | Toute personne a le droit d'avoir ses affaires traitées de manière impartiale, équitable et dans un délai raisonnable ; les autorités sont tenues de motiver leurs décisions. **Limite** : la motivation est constitutionnalisée, mais pas la fermeture de boucle procédurale. |
| **Luxembourg** | **Art. 29, Constitution** | Droit de pétition ; les autorités publiques sont tenues de répondre dans un délai raisonnable. **Limite** : le « délai raisonnable » reste indéterminé ; pas de sanction en cas de renvoi sans fin. |

### Codes of Administrative Procedure
| Pays | Instrument | Ce qu'il fait — et ses limites |
|---|---|---|
| **Suède** | **Loi sur la procédure administrative (2017:900)** | Codifie les principes de légalité, objectivité, proportionnalité, service, disponibilité. L'art. 49 permet à l'instance supérieure d'« ordonner à l'autorité qui a refusé de statuer de trancher la question dans un délai déterminé ». **Limite** : le recours est cassatoire (annulation), pas coercitif par mandamus ; pas de « condition de boucle » automatique. |
| **France** | **CRPA (Code des relations entre le public et l'administration)** | Art. L. 112-3 : accusé de réception. Art. 20 : transmission obligatoire. Art. 21 : silence vaut acceptation. **Limite** : le *ping-pong* entre administrations n'est pas une catégorie juridique ; le Défenseur des droits ne peut pas contraindre l'administration par voie juridictionnelle. |

### Soft Law: ReNEUAL Model Rules
Les *ReNEUAL Model Rules on EU Administrative Procedure* visent à codifier les principes communs de bonne administration en Europe. Elles pourraient conduire à une réforme juridique compatible avec le droit de l'Union dans les États membres. **Mais** : ce ne sont que des règles modèles, sans force contraignante. Aucun État membre ne les a transposées intégralement, et elles ne prévoient pas la *loop condition* ni l'audit technique indépendant.

### Les quatre ruptures du projet irlandais (sans équivalent en Europe)
| Mécanisme irlandais | Existe ailleurs en Europe ? | Commentaire |
|---|---|---|
| **La condition de boucle (3+ organismes déclinant sans statuer)** | **Non** | Aucun droit européen ne nomme ce phénomène structurel. Le renvoi sans fin entre administrations est un *non-droit*. |
| **L'Ombudsman *obligé* de saisir la Haute Cour** | **Non** | Les médiateurs nationaux émettent des recommandations ou des rapports spéciaux. Aucun n'a une obligation *mandatory* de saisine juridictionnelle coercitive. |
| **L'interdiction des affirmations de suffisance** | **Non** | Nulle part l'argument « nous connaissons déjà ce domaine » n'est expressément prohibé comme substitut à l'examen au fond. |
| **L'audit technique indépendant pour corpus testables** | **Non** | Aucune législation administrative européenne ne prévoit un panel d'auditeurs techniques sous l'égide du médiateur pour soumissions opérationnelles. |

## VII. The French Exposé des Motifs — Translation & Terminological Notes

### Key Translation Choices (Maintained Throughout)
| English Term | French Rendering | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Duty to determine** | *Obligation de statuer* | In French administrative law and CJEU vocabulary, *statuer* naturally conveys the legal obligation to render a reasoned decision. It distinguishes the procedural obligation from the discretionary content of the decision. |
| **Determination** | *Décision* or *décision motivée* | Systematic use of *détermination* would constitute an unnatural anglicism in a francophone legislative text. |
| **High Court** | *High Court* (untranslated) | Standard practice in French legal texts when referring to Irish jurisdictions. |
| **Mandamus** | *mandamus* (italicised) | A common-law remedy with no exact equivalent in civil law; kept in italics as a technical term. |
| **Loop condition** | *Condition de boucle* (with English gloss on first occurrence) | A technical concept proper to the Bill. Translating it as *cercle vicieux* or *boucle procédurale* would alter its conceptual scope, whereas the text seeks precisely to elevate this expression into an autonomous legal category. |

### Recommended Second Step
Once the full translation is complete, a harmonisation in the style of the **Conseil d'État français** and the **French versions of the European Commission** is recommended. This would ensure the document reads as if drafted directly in French — as a genuine *projet de loi* rather than a translation — significantly improving its institutional credibility.

## VIII. Conversational Toolkit — For Senators, Deputies, and Officials

### The Elevator Pitch (15 seconds)
> *"This Bill fixes a structural bug in Irish public administration. Right now, a citizen can submit material of genuine public value to the State, and every body involved can follow its own procedures correctly while nobody ever actually decides the matter. The Bill doesn't force anyone to agree with the submission. It simply removes the option of producing no determination at all."*

### Ready-to-Use Talking Points

**On the structural nature of the problem:**
> *"The Nullification Loop is not a conspiracy. It is an architectural property of a differentiated system where no single node is required to hold the aggregate obligation. The Bill fixes the architecture, not the personnel."*

**On the Ombudsman backstop:**
> *"The Ombudsman's mandatory High Court application in §10C is the backstop. It means the Loop cannot exhaust itself into silence. There is always a terminal node."*

**On the EU dimension:**
> *"This is directly relevant to Ireland's EU Council Presidency. The Commission's evaluation of Regulation 2021/821 opens in September 2026. Ireland's competent authority record — zero determinations across four transposition cycles — is an environmental fact that no Presidency chair can filter."*

**On the "Qualifying Submission" framing:**
> *"The 'Qualifying Submission' framing is deliberate. It separates the procedural question — does this meet the threshold? — from the normative question — is this good policy? That makes the Bill harder to oppose on partisan grounds."*

**On what the Bill does not do:**
> *"Nothing in this Bill requires a public body to accept, implement, fund, or adopt a submission. What a public body loses, under this Bill, is the option of producing no determination at all. The only argument against it is that the State should retain the right to produce no determination at all. That argument cannot be made in public."*

### Likely Objections & Responses

| Objection | Your Response |
|---|---|
| *"This will flood departments with vexatious correspondence."* | The definition of "qualifying submission" is tightly drawn. It requires particularity, statutory grounding, and a showing that no single body has independent incentive to evaluate. It is not a complaints charter. |
| *"This interferes with departmental autonomy."* | The Bill preserves full discretion over the *substance* of every determination. A body can still reject a submission. What it cannot do is produce no determination at all. |
| *"The Ombudsman shouldn't be dragged into court applications."* | The new function is conferred by statute under Article 15.2.1° — squarely within the Oireachtas's competence. The Ombudsman's constitutional independence is not compromised; the function is ancillary to its existing investigative role. |
| *"Retrospective application is constitutionally suspect."* | The Bill characterises the obligation as prospective (arising from commencement), not as a retrospective alteration of accrued rights. The authority is *In re Article 26 and the Health (Amendment) (No. 2) Bill* [2005]. |
| *"Isn't this just about one person's grievance?"* | The memoranda illustrate a general structural condition using a specific record. The Bill's provisions are drafted as general law. Any citizen with a qualifying submission would benefit. |

### The Ask (Tailored by Audience)

**If speaking to a Government TD or Minister:**
> *"Will the Government commit to not opposing this Bill on Second Stage, and to referring it to committee for substantive scrutiny rather than allowing it to lapse through procedural delay?"*

**If speaking to an Opposition or Independent Senator/TD:**
> *"Will you support the Bill on Second Stage, and if it reaches committee, will you seek amendments only to strengthen — not dilute — the five prohibited evasions and the Ombudsman's mandatory loop-closure function?"*

**If speaking to any member of the Oireachtas Joint Committee on EU Affairs or the PAC:**
> *"Will you request a briefing from the current competent authority (DETE) on the status of Article 9(2) assessment obligations accumulated before the August 2024 transfer, and on whether the Bill's §8C chain-of-duty provision would clarify the State's EU law compliance position ahead of the September 2026 Commission evaluation?"*

## IX. Glossary of Terms (Non-Terms of Art)

These terms appear in the Annex and in the Bill's introduction in a sense that is not standard legal usage. None of these definitions has legal effect.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Normative collapse** | The condition in which a system produces procedurally valid outputs while failing to discharge the substantive obligation those outputs purport to represent. The closest legal analogue is a nullity: an act valid in form but void in effect. |
| **Ontic collapse** | The condition in which the institutional subject capable of performing a required act no longer exists within the statutory architecture. The closest legal analogue is the situation addressed by *Re Anisminic* [1969]: an authority that acts in excess of its jurisdiction produces a nullity — but ontic collapse is the prior condition in which no authority with jurisdiction exists at all. |
| **Nullification loop** | The recursive pattern in which each body declines on grounds that the matter belongs elsewhere, while every other body has already declined, so that the obligation circulates without ever being held. The legal analogue is *forum non conveniens* run in a closed circuit: every forum is inconvenient, so the matter reaches no forum at all. |
| **Designed forgetting** | The deletion or suppression of formally lodged correspondence not for legitimate records management purposes but in order to extinguish the administrative record of a submission that has not been determined. The legal analogue is spoliation of evidence, applied to the administrative rather than the judicial record. |
| **Deferral machine** | A description of the nullification loop as a functional mechanism rather than a sequence of individual decisions. The loop produces non-determination as its stable output by distributing the cost of non-determination across enough nodes that no single node bears sufficient cost to make determination individually rational. |
| **Sovereign analytical capacity** | The State's independent ability to evaluate, integrate, and deploy methodology in the exercise of its statutory functions, without reliance on external frameworks whose objective functions may not align with the public interest. |
| **Recognition** (Kojèvian sense) | The acknowledgement of another as a subject capable of making claims that create obligations in the acknowledging party. The closest operative legal concept is standing. |
| **Silence veto** | The exercise of a power to determine the effective outcome of a submission through procedural silence: no reviewable act issues, and so no remedy attaches. |
| **Structural displacement** | The distribution of a statutory obligation across bodies in a configuration where no single body holds the full obligation required to discharge it. |
| **Sufficiency assertion** | The claim that the general discipline is known, that equivalent technologies exist, or that the body has general familiarity with the subject domain — substituted for actual engagement with the specific submitted corpus. |
| **Administrative amnesia** | The production of institutional forgetfulness whereby each new officer encounters the record as if for the first time. |
| **Foreclosure-by-Proxy** | A determination that cites conclusions formally rebutted and never defended, treating contested, unanswered assertions as settled authority. |
| **Pigouvian correction** | A statutory instrument that internalises an externality (the cost of non-determination, previously borne by the submitter and the public) into the decision-making calculus of the bodies that produce it. |
| **Condition de boucle / Loop condition** | The statutory name for the structural pattern where three or more public bodies have each declined to examine a qualifying submission on its merits without issuing a determination, and no body has accepted carriage of the obligation. |
| **Chain-of-duty** | The principle that the determination obligation persists through transfers of designation, ministerial reorganisation, or referral, with the clock running from first lodgement. |

## XI. The Ratio — Stated Plainly

> **What the Bill adds, that nothing else currently provides, is the word *cannot*.**
>
> A public body currently *can* produce no determination. Under this Act, it *cannot*.
>
> The reason this law exists is that the common good depends on the State being able to receive knowledge and do something with it. A State that can receive knowledge and extinguish it through procedural circulation has not merely failed a citizen — it has failed the common good that the knowledge was offered to serve.
>
> *Is sa Oireachtas atá cumhacht reachtaíochta an Stáit uile agus go heisiatach dílsithe: ní bheídh ag aon chomhlacht eile cumhacht dlíthe a dhéanamh don Stáit.*
> — Bunreacht na hÉireann, Airteagal 15.2.1°
