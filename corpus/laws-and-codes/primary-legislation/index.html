primary-legislation/

Acts and other primary legislative texts establishing powers, duties, institutions, and legal authority. Primary legislation is the originating normative layer: it confers competence on institutions, imposes duties on persons and bodies, defines categories of legal status, and establishes the procedural architecture through which administrative power is exercised, challenged, or remedied.

Texts in this directory are examined for what they constitute — the legal categories, competences, duties, exceptions, and remedies they bring into existence — and not only for what they command. The distinction matters analytically: a statutory duty may be formally constituted while remaining operatively unenforceable, inaccessible, or unenforced in the circumstances of interest.

Scope and selection

Included are Acts of the Oireachtas and, where applicable, pre-1922 legislation retained in force, that bear directly on the institutional conduct, administrative procedures, rights, duties, and remedies represented in the corpus. The selection is not exhaustive of Irish statute law; it is bounded by relevance to the analytical questions the corpus addresses.

Where an Act has been substantially amended, the version operative at the relevant time is the analytical reference point. Version history is noted in individual document entries where it bears on interpretation.

Analytical categories

Primary legislation typically establishes one or more of the following, which are tracked across entries:

  • Competence — the grant of power or authority to an institution or office-holder to act, decide, or direct;
  • Duty — an obligation imposed on an institution, office-holder, or person, whether absolute or qualified;
  • Discretion — a power whose exercise is left to the judgment of the competent actor, subject to stated or implied constraints;
  • Procedure — the required sequence of steps through which a power is validly exercised or a right vindicated;
  • Remedy — the mechanism through which a failure, breach, or wrong is addressable;
  • Exception — a carve-out from a duty, right, or prohibition, and the conditions on which it operates.

Documents

Index note: entries will be populated as source documents are added to this directory. Each entry records the instrument, its operative version, the analytical categories it engages, and any known gaps between its normative provisions and the documentary record of their operation.

FSD ontology: competence and duty

The FSD concepts of competence vacancy and duty displacement are most directly engaged by the primary legislation layer. A competence vacancy arises where a statutory power exists but the institution with authority to exercise it is absent, unwilling, or otherwise non-operative. Duty displacement arises where a statutory obligation exists but the mechanism through which it would be discharged has been structurally removed from the situation of the person to whom the duty is owed.

These are analytical classifications, not legal findings. Their conditions must be established against the documentary record.

Relationship to other layers

Primary legislation provides the authority base from which statutory instruments are made, EU obligations are transposed, and codes of practice derive their standing. Where a statutory instrument purports to exercise a power, or a code of practice claims to implement a duty, the relevant Act is the first point of reference for assessing whether that claim is well-founded.
