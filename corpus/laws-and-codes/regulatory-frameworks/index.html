regulatory-frameworks/

Composite regulatory frameworks that operate across or beneath the primary legislative layer: sector-specific regimes, multi-instrument schemes, and normative arrangements that do not fit cleanly within a single legislative instrument but constitute a coherent regulatory structure for a defined field of institutional conduct.

The category is necessary because regulatory reality does not always map onto the taxonomy of legal sources. A regulatory framework may be constituted by a combination of primary legislation, statutory instruments, EU obligations, administrative schemes, and non-statutory guidance, each contributing to the operative condition that governs conduct in a particular domain. Examining each instrument in isolation risks missing the structure within which they collectively function — or fail to function.

What qualifies as a regulatory framework

For the purposes of this directory, a regulatory framework is a structured arrangement of normative instruments that:

  • governs a defined field of institutional conduct or administrative activity;
  • comprises more than one instrument type (e.g. Act + SI, Act + EU regulation + code of practice);
  • establishes or implies an institutional architecture — bodies, competences, procedures, remedies — that is the operative vehicle for the framework's requirements;
  • can be examined as a coherent normative object for the purpose of assessing whether the architecture it establishes is operatively functional.

The ethics-in-public-office regime is one example: it is constituted by the Ethics in Public Office Acts, associated codes of conduct, the Standards in Public Office Commission, registration and declaration requirements, and the investigative and enforcement machinery that links these elements.

Analytical method

For each framework, the analysis proceeds in three stages:

  • Mapping: identify the instruments that constitute the framework, their interrelationships, and the institutional architecture they collectively establish;
  • Operative assessment: assess whether the institutional architecture is, or was at the relevant time, operatively functional — that is, whether the mechanisms through which the framework's requirements are given effect can be shown to have operated;
  • Gap analysis: identify the specific points at which normative provision and operative condition diverge, and characterise the nature and consequence of that divergence.

The coherence problem

A regulatory framework may be internally coherent as a normative text while being structurally incoherent as an institutional arrangement. Instruments may conflict. Competences may overlap or leave gaps. The body charged with enforcement may lack the powers to discharge its function, or may exercise discretion in ways that effectively remove the framework's protections from the persons it was designed to benefit.

These conditions — normative coherence without operative coherence — are among the central objects of FSD analysis. They are described and classified, not assumed to produce particular legal consequences.

Evidence gap: mapping a regulatory framework is an analytical exercise; it does not establish how institutions within that framework actually behaved. Claims about framework operation require documentary support from the administrative record.

FSD ontology: structural failure and normative collapse

The regulatory-frameworks layer is the primary site for the FSD concepts of structural failure and normative collapse. Structural failure denotes the condition in which an institutional architecture, while formally constituted, cannot be shown to have been capable of producing the operative conditions the framework requires. Normative collapse denotes the more severe condition in which the gap between normative provision and institutional reality is such that the framework's protections are effectively unavailable to those within its scope. These classifications require careful evidential grounding and should not be applied without establishing their specific conditions against the documentary record.
