laws-and-codes/

Normative source material. Legislation, regulations, codes, and related instruments that constitute the legal and regulatory frameworks within which institutional action is represented as authorised, required, constrained, or prohibited. These texts provide the normative reference layer against which institutional representations and observable behaviour may subsequently be compared.

The distinction is deliberate: a normative text prescribes, authorises, constrains, or defines; it does not by itself establish that the corresponding institutional behaviour occurred.

Subdirectories

  • primary-legislation/ — Acts and other primary legislative texts establishing powers, duties, institutions, and legal authority
  • statutory-instruments/ — regulations, orders, and other instruments made under delegated legislative authority
  • eu-regulations/ — directly applicable EU regulations and associated instruments relevant to the corpus
  • eu-directives/ — EU directives establishing obligations or frameworks requiring implementation or transposition
  • codes-of-practice/ — formal codes, guidance, and procedural standards bearing on institutional conduct

Semiotic relevance

These texts are examined not only for what they prescribe, but for the categories through which legal and administrative reality is constituted: competence, obligation, discretion, authority, procedure, responsibility, exception, remedy, and enforcement.

Their language establishes distinctions between what is permitted and prohibited, required and discretionary, competent and incompetent, concluded and unresolved. Such distinctions become analytically significant when institutional representations or observable behaviour are compared with the normative framework they invoke or operate within.

The justice-facing void

The laws-and-codes layer also marks a particular analytical problem: a normative framework may be formally present while the institutional mechanisms through which its protections, duties, procedures, or remedies are made operative are absent, fragmented, displaced, or otherwise ineffective.

The resulting question is not simply whether a law or code exists. It is whether the normative condition it establishes is capable of becoming an operative institutional condition for those who stand on the receiving side of the administrative system.

This repository therefore distinguishes between normative validity and operative effect. A formally valid provision does not, by its existence alone, establish that the institution performed the act, reached the determination, provided the remedy, or discharged the duty contemplated by that provision.

The FSD ontology emerged from this justice-facing void: an attempt to formalise concepts needed to describe the gap between normative architecture and its operation. It is consequently an analytical artefact arising from the corpus, not a substitute for the underlying legal texts or documentary evidence.

Evidence discipline

Documents in this directory remain source material. Analysis must distinguish:

  • normative proposition — what the text provides, requires, permits, prohibits, or defines;
  • institutional representation — how an actor represents or invokes that normative framework;
  • observable behaviour — what the documentary record establishes that an institution actually did;
  • operative condition — whether the institutional mechanism required to give effect to the norm can be shown to have operated.

A failure of operative effect should not automatically be characterised as illegality, negligence, bad faith, or intentional obstruction. Those are further propositions requiring their own evidence.

Evidence gap: the presence of a normative text does not establish its interpretation, application, enforcement, or practical effect. Claims concerning institutional behaviour require corroborating documentary evidence from the relevant administrative record.

Analytical relationship

The directory therefore provides one side of a comparison:

NORMATIVE TEXT
↓
prescribes / authorises / constrains / prohibits
↓
INSTITUTIONAL OPERATION
↓
performed / deferred / referred / omitted / unresolved
↓
OPERATIVE EFFECT

The analytical task is to determine, from the evidence, whether and how these layers correspond, diverge, or fail to connect.

FSD ontology: analytical response to the normative–operative gap

The FSD ontology provides proposed formal concepts for describing particular forms of non-determination, structural failure, normative collapse, operative-condition failure, and related phenomena. These concepts are hypotheses or analytical classifications unless and until their conditions are established against the documentary record.
