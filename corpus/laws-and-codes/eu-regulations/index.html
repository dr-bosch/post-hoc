eu-regulations/

Directly applicable EU regulations relevant to the corpus. EU regulations are binding in their entirety and apply directly in all Member States without requiring transposition into national law. Their direct applicability means that no implementing national instrument is required for their normative force to be engaged — though the institutional machinery through which their protections, rights, or obligations are made operationally effective remains a domestic question.

This distinction — between direct applicability as a matter of EU law and operative effect as a matter of institutional practice — is analytically significant. A regulation may be directly applicable while remaining effectively inaccessible to the persons it is intended to protect, or while the domestic institutions charged with its application are absent, under-resourced, or non-operative.

Scope and selection

Included are EU regulations bearing on the subject matter of the corpus, including instruments in the fields of data protection, fundamental rights, administrative procedure, and any sector-specific regulatory frameworks directly engaged. Selection is bounded by relevance; this directory is not a survey of EU regulatory law.

Where a regulation has been amended, repealed, or replaced during the relevant period, the version operative at the material time is the reference point. The distinction between the applying regulation and any comitology or delegated acts made under it is preserved where it bears on the analysis.

Direct effect and Member State obligations

EU regulations typically impose obligations on Member States as well as on private and institutional actors. Where a regulation imposes an obligation on Ireland — whether to establish a supervisory authority, to provide an effective remedy, to ensure independence, or to take other institutional steps — the question of whether that obligation has been discharged is an empirical question about institutional conduct, not answered by the existence of the regulation itself.

The analytical task is to identify:

  • what the regulation requires of Member State institutions;
  • what domestic institutional arrangement has been established in purported compliance;
  • whether the documentary record supports the conclusion that the arrangement operates as the regulation contemplates;
  • where there is a gap, what its nature and consequence are.

Relationship to national law

Where EU regulations interact with or displace national law, the relationship is noted. The primacy of EU law over inconsistent national provisions is established doctrine, but the practical consequence — which norm governs in a given situation — requires case-by-case analysis rather than an assumption of displacement.

Evidence gap: the direct applicability of a regulation does not establish that the institution applied it, was aware of its applicability, or that effective enforcement mechanisms were operative. These are factual questions requiring documentary support.

FSD ontology: normative validity without operative effect

EU regulations present the sharpest instance of the normative validity / operative effect distinction. A regulation may be formally valid, directly applicable, and confer rights on individuals, while the domestic institutional conditions required to make those rights practically claimable are absent. This condition — valid norm, inoperative mechanism — is a central object of FSD analysis.
