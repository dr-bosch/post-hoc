# **I. SUMMARY STATEMENT**

This document records a statutory obligation under EU Regulation 2021/821 (the recast Dual-Use Regulation) that has not been discharged across nineteen years, spanning five governments, six Taoisigh, and two successive national competent authority designations. It is submitted not as policy advocacy but as a documented evidentiary record for institutional recipients with the standing to act on it.

The originating failure is this: since May 2006, a corpus of Operations Research material with documented dual-use characteristics has been presented to Irish authorities. The Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFA) held the national competent authority designation under EU Regulation 428/2009 and subsequently Regulation 2021/821 from 2009 until August 22nd, 2024\. Across that full period of designation, it produced **zero evaluations** and **zero statutory determinations**. The material was received, retained, and never assessed.

On August 22nd, 2024, the Control of Exports Act 2023 commenced, transferring the competent authority designation to the Department of Enterprise, Trade and Employment (DETE). The transfer was executed without audit of the accumulated obligation, without discharge of the unperformed duty, and without any mechanism requiring the receiving authority to assess material held by the transferring authority. The accumulated obligation was not extinguished by the transfer. It was structurally abandoned.

The transfer did not produce a modernised competent authority. It produced a competent authority function fragmented across three institutions —DETE (licensing), DFA (international regime memberships), and the Department of Defence (security assessment architecture) —with no statutory coordination mechanism between them. The function EU Regulation 2021/821 requires —an integrated assessment with simultaneous access to the commercial export dimension and the security application dimension —is **architecturally impossible** within the current Irish structure. This is the operative compliance failure before the Commission's mandatory evaluation of Regulation 2021/821, which opens between September 10th, 2026 and September 10th, 2028\.

Ireland assumed the Council Presidency on **July 1st, 2026**. In that capacity, the Irish State now chairs Council proceedings on dual-use technology controls, rule of law, and civil protection coordination. The structural conditions documented in this record will enter the Commission's formal evaluation record regardless of domestic resolution.

*The breach of fiduciary duty is widespread. The ethical breach is clear. The security breaches are manifold. The duty to reason is repeatedly neglected; the impasse is manifest, active, and compounding. The duty of care is undischarged. It has been handed from minister to minister, from government to government, without a single statutory determination being issued —while material directly relevant to national security has been ignored at every level of the executive and legislature simultaneously. Ireland assumes the Council Presidency on July 1st, 2026\. The Commission's evaluation of Regulation 2021/821 opens September 10th, 2026\. This record is publicly indexed and transmitted to European institutional recipients.*

### **II.2 — The No-Interregnum Axiom: Article 28 and the Constitutional Continuity of Duty**

Article 28.6 of Bunreacht na hÉireann provides that every Minister holds office at the Taoiseach's discretion and may be removed at any time. Article 28.4.1° makes the Government collectively responsible to Dáil Éireann for all executive action. These provisions, read together with the foundational principle of administrative law that obligations run with functions and not with officeholders, produce what this record terms the **No-Interregnum Axiom**:

*A statutory or regulatory obligation, once crystallised in respect of a public function, is not extinguished, interrupted, or suspended by any change in the identity of the officeholder discharging that function, by any transfer or reallocation of the function between departments, or by any restructuring of the ministerial portfolio in which the function is held.*

The Axiom is not a new legal proposition. It is the administrative law principle that obligations travel with the function, stated with precision for application to the chain of duty documented in this record. Its constitutional basis is threefold.

**First: Article 28.4.1°** — collective executive responsibility. The Government as a body is collectively accountable to the Dáil for every act of every member. Ministerial rotation, coalition reshuffles, and the reallocation of portfolios are internal executive arrangements. They do not alter the external legal relationship between the State and a person to whom a statutory obligation is owed. The obligation that crystallised in 2006 against the Irish State did not change character in 2011 when the DFA portfolio was restructured, in 2022 when Martin moved to the Tánaiste role, in 2024 when the CA designation transferred to DETE, or in 2025 when the current Government was formed. In each case the State remained the obligor; the function changed address within the State; the duty did not lapse.

**Second: Article 28.6** — ministerial tenure at Taoiseach's discretion. The power of removal confirms that ministerial office is a delegated function of the executive, not a personal entitlement. A minister does not own the function; she holds it on behalf of the State. When she leaves, the function —and any unperformed obligation attached to it— passes to her successor. The Tánaiste who held Foreign Affairs and Defence simultaneously at the moment of the August 22nd, 2024 CA transfer did not extinguish the unperformed obligation by failing to refer the record to DETE. He passed it, unacknowledged, to a successor architecture that did not know it existed. The obligation survived the transfer. The Axiom requires that the receiving authority be treated as having inherited it.

**Third: the No-Interregnum corollary for the High Court proceedings** — mandamus runs against the constitutional officer in post. It is not defeated by a plea that the obligation arose under a predecessor's tenure. The Taoiseach is named in the mandamus application as the head of government in whom the unperformed 2009 ministerial commitment —made through his own ministerial office— is now personally vested. The argument that a prime ministerial reshuffling of portfolios in the intervening sixteen years extinguishes personal liability is not available where the same individual has held each of the successive offices through which the obligation has passed.

*The No-Interregnum Axiom is the constitutional bridge between the tortfeasor taxonomy in Section II.1 and the High Court application. Groups A through D describe who held the obligation and when. The Axiom explains why none of the transfers between them extinguished it.*

That question is now before courts and institutions whose processes cannot be resolved by administrative filing. The Commission's mandatory evaluation of Regulation 2021/821 will assess Ireland's compliance record as a regulatory fact. The High Court, acting under Order 84 RSC, can compel the performance of the obligation by mandamus. Neither mechanism requires the domestic administrative architecture to function as designed. Both operate precisely because it has not.

There is also the AI Office dimension. Minister of State Niamh Smyth and CEO Paul Byrne of the AI Office of Ireland have now been briefed of the *Interregnum Nullificans*, the DOD Foreclosure, and the overarching due diligence that remains undischarged in its wake. This is not incidental to the record. DETE simultaneously holds the competent authority designation under Regulation 2021/821 and houses the AI Office —the institutional body whose stated mandate is coordinated, technically competent oversight across Ireland's designated competent authorities. The structural gap this record documents in the dual-use CA architecture is the same gap the AI Office's own mandate presupposes has been resolved. It has not.

### **II.5 — The Ethics: Coordination or Architecture?**

A preliminary caution is necessary before characterising the actors named above as custodians of a known ethical breach. The register maps institutional receipt, not individual comprehension. An auto-reply confirms that a system received correspondence; it does not confirm that any officer read, understood, or evaluated it. It is entirely possible that officers across these departments and offices processed the correspondence according to their own bounded domain **and found, within that domain, nothing that compelled further action** without understanding what was being submitted or why it engaged the obligations it did.

The duty to determine does not depend on comprehension. A body that receives a qualifying submission has an obligation to route it to qualified assessors regardless of whether its own officers understand the submission's substance. The ethical breach in each group is the failure to route —not the failure to understand. This formulation is both more defensible and more damaging: it requires no finding of bad faith, and it forecloses the deflection that the submission was too complex to evaluate.

The register appears to document coordinated silence. It does not. What it documents is the output of Herbert Simon's satisficing applied at institutional scale: each actor terminates search at the first acceptable outcome within their bounded domain. No actor's domain returns "determination." The aggregate is indistinguishable from coordination —but requires no coordination to produce.

This distinction matters ethically and legally. Coordinated silence implies shared intent, which requires proof. Architectural silence (e.g. as produced by a system of differentiated functions with no coordination requirement) requires no such proof. The ethical breach is not that these actors conspired. It is that the architecture permitted each to act locally within their procedural code while collectively producing a result that none would individually defend: twenty years of non-determination on a submitted corpus of public value, across fourteen formal acknowledgment events and two competent authority designations.

The legislative mark the ethical breach misses is the Qualifying Submissions (Duty to Determine) Bill 2026's precise target. The Bill requires no finding of intent. It requires only that the pattern —three or more bodies declining without determination over five years— is established as a structural fact. The register above satisfies that condition on its face, without requiring any finding of coordination between the actors named within it.

The question before the record is therefore not whether these actors acted in concert. It is whether the architecture that permitted this result should be permitted to continue —and whether any actor in the register, having now received this document, can claim the bounded-domain justification that the Nullification Loop depends upon.

### **II.6 — Vicarious State Responsibility**

Memoranda D and E of the formal complaint record developed the concept of **Vicarious State Responsibility** as a structural feature of how the Irish State has handled the diffusion of accountability across this record. The concept has a precise doctrinal content in this context.

Vicarious State Responsibility operates where responsibility is continuously referred upward, laterally, or backward through an institutional architecture —without any node in the architecture accepting the substantive obligation. It is structurally distinct from the doctrine of respondeat superior (under which an employer bears liability for an employee's acts within the scope of employment): that doctrine allocates liability downward from principal to agent. The mechanism documented in this record runs in the opposite direction (e.g. each actor refers the obligation upward to a minister, laterally to another department, or backward to a prior officeholder) with the effect that responsibility is diffused to the point of disappearance.

The critical legal property of this mechanism is that diffusion does not extinguish the obligation. It relocates the breach. Where the State is the obligor —as it is under EU Regulation 2021/821 and under Articles 40.3 and 28.4.1° of Bunreacht na hÉireann— the State cannot discharge its obligations by operating an internal referral architecture that ensures no single actor holds the complete obligation. The State remains liable at the systemic level even where every individual node within the system has, in its own bounded terms, complied with its procedural code.

This is the constitutional import of Article 28.4.1°, read through the lens of the No-Interregnum Axiom. The Government is collectively responsible to the Dáil for all executive action —including the collective non-action of its constituent departments. A Government that has operated an architecture of diffusion for twenty years, across five administrations, remains the State against which mandamus runs. The individual officeholders who held the function at each stage are the named links in the tortfeasor chain; the State itself is the corporate respondent whose vicarious liability the chain establishes.

Memorandum E characterised the architecture of diffusion as a form of **recursive legitimacy maintenance**: a system that preserves the outward form of governmental responsiveness while structurally precluding the substance. The system absorbs the signal, generates acknowledgements, and produces no evaluative output. This is not a breakdown of the institutional process. It is the institutional process performing its most durable function: the conversion of civic obligation into procedural motion, indefinitely.

The legal consequence is stark. Where an obligation runs against the State (as a regulatory obligation under EU law and as a constitutional obligation under Bunreacht na hÉireann) the State's internal architecture of diffusion cannot defeat the obligation. The appropriate respondent in the mandamus application is not the most recent officeholder. It is the constitutional office in which the unperformed obligation is currently vested. That office is the Taoiseach's, by virtue of Article 28.4.1°, Article 28.6, and the No-Interregnum Axiom.

**VII. WHY DOMESTIC REMEDIES ARE STRUCTURALLY INSUFFICIENT**

The Ombudsman complaint, the parliamentary committee declinations, the Clerk's Section 8(3) determination of September 10th, 2025 —these are not failures of the individuals involved. They are outputs of systems that process inputs according to their own internal codes.

In the theoretical framework developed by Niklas Luhmann, legal and administrative systems are autopoietic: they reproduce themselves by processing communications in forms their own architecture can handle, without necessary reference to the substantive condition the input describes. Each domestic oversight body —the Ombudsman, the committee clerks, the Cathaoirligh— processes the complaint according to its own code: jurisdiction, standing, time limits, admissibility. A technicality is sufficient. The system produces a determination that is internally valid. The substantive question remains outside every domestic system's operational boundary.

This is not a conspiracy. It is a structural feature of differentiated functional systems: each system discharges its own procedural obligation without any system discharging the underlying substantive one. The result is a **nullification loop** that is self-sustaining, personnel-independent, and formally valid at every stage. It has operated through six Taoisigh and four governments without change of output.

*The Commission's evaluation of Regulation 2021/821 operates from outside this boundary. It receives the Member State compliance record as an environmental fact, not as a communication to be processed according to domestic procedural rules. The European Ombudsman, the CJEU, and the PETI Committee operate at the same systemic level. These are the mechanisms through which the substantive question reaches an institution structurally positioned to examine it.*

Mandamus ordinarily runs against a public body or constitutional officer in that capacity. The High Court has the power, in appropriate proceedings, to require a person who received a personal briefing and gave a personal undertaking to give evidence as to its content.
