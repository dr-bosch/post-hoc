**I.**

# **Summary Statement**

This document records a statutory obligation under EU Regulation 2021/821 (the recast Dual-Use Regulation) that has not been discharged across nineteen years, spanning five governments, six Taoisigh, and two successive national competent authority designations. It is submitted not as policy advocacy but as a documented evidentiary record for institutional recipients with the standing to act on it.

The originating failure is this: since May 2006, a corpus of Operations Research material with documented dual-use characteristics has been presented to Irish authorities. The Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade (DFA) held the national competent authority designation under EU Regulation 428/2009 and subsequently Regulation 2021/821 from 2009 until August 22nd, 2024. Across that full period of designation, it produced **zero evaluations** and **zero statutory determinations**. The material was received, retained, and never assessed.

On August 22nd, 2024, the Control of Exports Act 2023 commenced, transferring the competent authority designation to the Department of Enterprise, Trade and Employment (DETE). The transfer was executed without audit of the accumulated obligation, without discharge of the unperformed duty, and without any mechanism requiring the receiving authority to assess material held by the transferring authority. The accumulated obligation was not extinguished by the transfer. It was structurally abandoned.

The transfer did not produce a modernised competent authority. It produced a competent authority function fragmented across three institutions ---DETE (licensing), DFA (international regime memberships), and the Department of Defence (security assessment architecture) ---with no statutory coordination mechanism between them. The function EU Regulation 2021/821 requires ---an integrated assessment with simultaneous access to the commercial export dimension and the security application dimension ---is **architecturally impossible** within the current Irish structure. This is the operative compliance failure before the Commission\'s mandatory evaluation of Regulation 2021/821, which opens between September 10th, 2026 and September 10th, 2028.

Ireland assumed the Council Presidency on **July 1st, 2026**. In that capacity, the Irish State now chairs Council proceedings on dual-use technology controls, rule of law, and civil protection coordination. The structural conditions documented in this record will enter the Commission\'s formal evaluation record regardless of domestic resolution.

# **The Transfer as Structural Escape: Fragmentation, DTCI, and the Commission Evaluation Window**

The Control of Exports Act 2023 commenced on August 22nd, 2024, transferring the national competent authority designation under EU Regulation 2021/821 from the Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade to the Department of Enterprise, Trade and Employment. The procedural modernisation was valid: legislation passed, commencement order made, new authority designated.

What none of these procedural outputs addressed was the substance. The DFA had held the designation for eighteen years. Across that period it produced **zero evaluations** and **zero statutory determinations** on the material formally submitted since May 2006. The Control of Exports Act 2023 did not require the DFA to audit the accumulated obligation before transfer, did not require the DFA to transmit the unprocessed record to DETE, and created no mechanism compelling DETE to assess material in the DFA's custody.

The Tánaiste held both portfolios at the moment of transfer. He did not progress the outstanding obligation, did not refer the unprocessed record to the incoming authority, and issued no ministerial determination. The accumulated obligation was **not transferred** with the designation. It was structurally abandoned. This is the **Silence Veto** applied at the moment of structural transition.

The transfer did not produce a unified competent authority. It produced a competent authority function distributed across three institutions:

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **DETE**               Holds the CA designation and administers the Export Authorisation System. Oversees licensing and exporter compliance ---but only for commercial export applications, not for evaluation of a submitted research corpus.
  ---------------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **DFA**                Retains Ireland's memberships in the Wassenaar Arrangement, the Nuclear Suppliers Group, the MTCR, and the Australia Group ---the treaty-level frameworks within which CA assessments are situated. No longer the competent authority.

  **Dept. of Defence**   Retains the security and military application assessment architecture ---civil defence, the OEP, the interdepartmental security environment assessment process. Has no role in DETE's licensing workflow.
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

None of these three institutions is formally required to consult either of the others before a licensing determination is made. Dual-use is dual because it requires both dimensions ---commercial export and security application--- to be assessed together. The current Irish structure assesses one dimension per institution, in separate buildings, under separate ministers, **with no coordination requirement**. The Commission's January 2024 White Paper on Export Controls identified inconsistent implementation, divergent national control lists, and a lack of coordinated political oversight as the primary EU-wide compliance failures. Ireland's post-transfer architecture is a precise institutional embodiment of all three simultaneously.

The Defence Technology Cluster Ireland has been established as a national coordination platform for civilian protection, critical infrastructure resilience, and emergency-response innovation ---the precise domain the O.R. submissions of 2006 addressed. No public record connects the DTCI's establishment to the nineteen-year unprocessed record.

## **1. The Byrne Paradox: A Remit That Maps the Fragmentation Without Resolving It**

Thomas Byrne holds the position of Minister of State ---an operational role, not a cabinet seat. His remit spans European Affairs and Defence across three departments simultaneously: the Department of the Taoiseach, the Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade, and the Department of Defence. He chairs the Interdepartmental Committee on EU Engagement and has written to all ministerial colleagues underlining the importance of timely transposition. On its face, this is precisely the brief that should have surfaced the unperformed CA obligation under Regulation 2021/821.

It has not. The reason is sequential and technical. The Department of the Taoiseach provides the political coordination layer. The Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade retains the Wassenaar Arrangement, the Nuclear Suppliers Group, the MTCR, and the Australia Group regime memberships ---the treaty-level frameworks within which any CA assessment must be situated. The Department of Defence retains the security assessment architecture: civil defence, the Office of Emergency Planning, and the interdepartmental security environment assessment process. These three departments together hold the *preconditions* for a CA assessment. They do not hold the CA function itself.

The CA designation ---the statutory authority to evaluate dual-use items and issue determinations under Regulation 2021/821--- was transferred to the Department of Enterprise, Trade and Employment on August 22nd, 2024. DETE is not within Byrne's remit. It reports to a separate cabinet minister. The consequence is precise: Byrne holds the preconditions for an integrated assessment across three departments; the authority to conduct that assessment sits in a fourth department he cannot direct. The integrated function the Regulation requires cannot be assembled from within his brief.

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  *As Minister of State, Byrne is privy to cabinet strategy but does not hold a cabinet seat. The operational matters of enterprise within and without the EU ---export licensing, dual-use compliance, trade regulation, and the Article 26 reporting obligation to the Commission--- are distributed across no fewer than four cabinet-level ministers: the Taoiseach, the Tánaiste, the Minister for Enterprise Trade and Employment, and the Minister for Foreign Affairs and Trade. Byrne sits at the intersection of their remits without holding any of them. The power vacuum is not adjacent to his position. **VII.**

# **Why Domestic Remedies Are Structurally Insufficient**

The Ombudsman complaint, the parliamentary committee declinations, the Clerk's Section 8(3) determination of September 10th, 2025 ---these are not failures of the individuals involved. They are outputs of systems that process inputs according to their own internal codes.

In the theoretical framework developed by Niklas Luhmann, legal and administrative systems are autopoietic: they reproduce themselves by processing communications in forms their own architecture can handle, without necessary reference to the substantive condition the input describes. Each domestic oversight body ---the Ombudsman, the committee clerks, the Cathaoirligh--- processes the complaint according to its own code: jurisdiction, standing, time limits, admissibility. A technicality is sufficient. The system produces a determination that is internally valid. The substantive question remains outside every domestic system's operational boundary.

This is not a conspiracy. It is a structural feature of differentiated functional systems: each system discharges its own procedural obligation without any system discharging the underlying substantive one. The result is a **nullification loop** that is self-sustaining, personnel-independent, and formally valid at every stage. It has operated through six Taoisigh and four governments without change of output.
